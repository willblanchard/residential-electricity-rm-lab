The model is directional, not a rate filing.

The dashboard is useful because it makes the economics explicit. It is still a class-project model: WTP is proxied, leakage is simulated, and regulatory constraints are simplified. Future work should replace those assumptions with observed customer and utility data.

1

Simulated customer data

ResStock/NLR load profiles are modeled rather than observed utility meter data, so actual behavior under unusual weather or prices may differ.

2

One-year demand sample

The analysis uses one simulated year. A longer historical meter record would better estimate rare peaks and customer-specific variability.

3

Annual bill behavior

Customers respond to annual cost in the source model, not to seasonal, hourly, or peak-period bill salience. That weakens the modeled value of segmentation.

4

WTP scaling

The WTP curves and ResStock consumption data live on different scales, so the model applies a scaling bridge before interpreting demand response.

5

Regulatory realism

Bill protection, fairness constraints, and filing politics are simplified relative to a real utility proceeding.

6

Cost proxy

Utility costs use a transparent wholesale-energy plus capacity-cost proxy, not a utility-specific avoided-cost study.

Future work

The most valuable extension is empirical calibration: actual customer enrollment, measured load response, and utility-specific cost curves would turn the current mechanism comparison into a deployable pricing recommendation.

Customer plan choice Let customers self-select into rate plans and test adverse selection against the default fixed-rate pool.
Appliance-specific rates Separate rooftop solar, batteries, EVs, and controllable HVAC because each has a different load shape.
Commercial extension Add commercial customers to capture system-wide cost allocation beyond the residential portfolio.
Implementation costs Include billing upgrades, customer communication, metering needs, and transition costs in the optimization.
