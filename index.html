flat vs TOU vs demand-charge pricing

Phase 1 Phase 2 Phase 3 WTP

Lower revenue can still be better revenue.

A utility can file TOU or demand-charge rates as revenue-neutral for a passive customer base, then see realized economics change as customers respond. This case study compares that defensible filing baseline with adoption scenarios where elastic behavior, smart thermostats, and home batteries reduce high-cost evening energy and scarce grid capacity.

File rates as neutral.

Flat, TOU, and demand-charge designs start from the same passive baseline, matching the revenue-neutral argument a utility would bring to a regulator.

2

Let adoption change load.

Passive behavior, elastic behavior, thermostats, and batteries then reshape the same high-peak-day end-use profile, revealing how the filed rate performs after customers respond.

3

Compare revenue to margin.

The managerial question is whether lost customer revenue is more than offset by avoided evening energy and capacity exposure.

Executive finding

Start with a rate plan that is defensible as revenue-neutral for passive customers. Then let the same homes adopt smart controls.

Rate-plan economics per 10,000 homes

TOU and demand-charge designs are calibrated to be revenue-neutral on an all-inelastic baseline, approximating the PUC defense a utility would need. Then the model lets device adoption erode revenue while avoiding high-cost energy and capacity.

Tariff optimization layer

A small grid-search solver chooses TOU spread or demand-charge levels that maximize gross margin across normalized building demand profiles while preserving the revenue-neutral filing baseline, keeping realized revenue close enough to flat, and staying under a portfolio peak-capacity screen. Demand-curve WTP calibration is separated as Phase 3 work.

This is a stylized case-study model for the final paper: rates are calibrated to a passive baseline, load response is simulated from NLR/OEDI end-use profiles and normalized building-demand profile shapes, and costs use an explicit wholesale-energy plus capacity proxy. Static WattShift rate-plan, price-signal, and bill-estimate CSVs were generated by a one-off pull without storing API credentials; current interactive scenario outputs are available through the export buttons above, including the tariff-optimization candidate grid. A separate Phase 2 extension isolates optional-rate choice, population leakage, and adverse selection from this universal-tariff comparison.
