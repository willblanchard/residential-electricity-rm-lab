The model uses one fixed population across the pricing tests.

The results pages start from the same normalized 10,000-home portfolio. The population is a scenario design, not a census claim: it crosses behavior type with device ownership so the rate designs face passive homes, price-aware homes, and homes with automation.

Shares are scaled to 10,000 homes so revenue, cost, margin, kWh, and peak can be compared on the same base.

Why this population exists

The portfolio is designed to separate three effects that are easy to mix up: human price response, automated device response, and rate-menu choice. Single Price Results uses the population as a fixed mix; Parallel Options Results then asks which customers leave the fixed pool when optional plans are offered.

Same base Flat, TOU, and demand-charge cases all start from this population.
Controllable mix The Phase 1 allocation controls can stress-test the shares without redefining the buckets.
Choice is separate Opt-in behavior belongs to Parallel Options Results, not the baseline population definition.

Passive / inelastic No energy devices 20% 2,000 Non-controlled load does not shift. Baseline revenue anchor for customers with no flexibility.
Passive / inelastic Just thermostat 10% 1,000 HVAC can respond automatically, but human usage stays fixed. Tests automation without assuming price-aware habits.
Passive / inelastic Just battery 5% 500 Battery dispatch is accepted only when it lowers the customer bill. Isolates storage economics from thermostat response.

Somewhat elastic No energy devices 15% 1,500 Non-controlled load shifts modestly under price pressure. Captures human response without smart-home automation.
Somewhat elastic Just thermostat 15% 1,500 Elastic usage combines with HVAC automation. Represents the common smart-thermostat segment.
Somewhat elastic Just battery 5% 500 Elastic usage combines with bill-saving battery dispatch. Tests whether storage value survives customer response.
Somewhat elastic Thermostat + battery 20% 2,000 Behavioral shift, HVAC response, and battery dispatch all apply. Largest flexible segment and the strongest stress test for rate design.
